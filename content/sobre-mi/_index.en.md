## About

I am a historian and philosopher whose research lies at the intersection of contemporary philosophy, phenomenology, aesthetics, philosophy of technology, and legal theory. My work is concerned primarily with the processes through which individuals, collectives, institutions, and forms of life come into being, transform themselves, and persist.

My academic formation began in History and Aesthetics, where I became interested in cultural forms as historical, material, and relational processes rather than as static structures. I hold a degree in History and a Master’s degree in Aesthetics from the National University of Colombia. I subsequently pursued my philosophical training at the Pontifical Catholic University of Chile, where I obtained a Master’s degree and a PhD in Philosophy.

A significant part of my research has focused on the problem of individuation and its implications for our understanding of existence, subjectivity, and relation. Within this trajectory, the philosophies of Gilbert Simondon and Jan Patočka have been fundamental interlocutors. Their work makes it possible to shift attention away from the constituted individual toward the processes, relations, and conditions through which individuation becomes possible.

My engagement with both authors has also involved translation. I have translated texts by Gilbert Simondon and Jan Patočka, an activity that forms part of my philosophical engagement with their work and of my broader interest in the transmission and circulation of contemporary philosophy.

From this perspective, my research also addresses questions concerning technology, identity, institutions, and forms of shared existence. I am particularly interested in how what we recognize as a unity—an individual, an identity, an institution, or a form of life—emerges from relations that precede it and that continue to transform it.

My current research develops these questions by bringing phenomenology, the philosophy of individuation, philosophy of technology, and legal theory into dialogue with broader problems in contemporary philosophy.
