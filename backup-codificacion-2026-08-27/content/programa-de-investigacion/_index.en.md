## Existing in the Problem

An inquiry into the conditions through which something comes into being, persists, transforms itself, and ceases to be what it was.

### 01 — Problematicity

Problematicity constitutes the point of departure of my research programme. A problem is not simply a question that remains unresolved, but a field of tensions, incompatibilities, and potentials from which new forms of existence may emerge. To investigate problematicity is therefore to shift attention from already constituted entities toward the conditions under which a problem can be oriented, transformed, and resolved, giving rise to new configurations of existence.

### 02 — Individuation

Individuation is the mode through which problems are resolved. It is not the realization of a previously determined identity, but the process through which a metastable field acquires consistency by finding a provisional resolution to the tensions that traverse it. This line of research investigates how individuals, collectives, objects, and forms of existence emerge through processes that transform the very field from which they arise.

### 03 — Ontogenetic Practices

Ontogenetic practices are practices through which forms of existence are produced, maintained, transformed, and oriented toward possible resolutions of the problems in which they are involved. From this perspective, ethics is not limited to choosing between already established norms or values. It concerns the conditions under which modes of existence and relations are produced, as well as the ways in which problems are oriented toward their resolution while sustaining new possibilities of individuation.

### 04 — Historical Regimes of Individuation

Individuation does not occur outside history. Each epoch organizes particular ways of identifying, articulating, and resolving its problems through specific ontogenetic practices. Bodies, identities, institutions, forms of life, and modes of subjectivation thus emerge within historically situated regimes that establish both the possibilities of existence and the directions in which problems may be resolved. This line investigates these historical regimes and the transformations they undergo when their modes of resolution become inadequate.

### 05 — Technique

Technique is not merely a collection of instruments designed to satisfy human needs. Technical objects, systems, infrastructures, and material environments participate in the configuration of relations, capacities, and modes of existence. This line investigates the ontogenetic dimension of technique: the ways in which technical arrangements orient problems, open or close fields of potential resolution, and transform individuals, collectives, and worlds.

### 06 — Epistemology

Knowledge is not produced solely through the relation between a subject and an already constituted object. Knowing itself emerges through practices that identify problems, construct objects, establish distinctions, and determine legitimate modes of resolution. This line investigates the historical, material, technical, and institutional conditions through which problems become knowable, knowledge becomes valid, and subjects and objects of knowledge are constituted.

### 07 — Pedagogy

Pedagogy can be understood as a practice for transforming the capacities through which individuals participate in a shared world and respond to its problems. This line investigates the material, technical, social, and conceptual conditions of formation, focusing on how learning reorganizes capacities, opens new possibilities of action, and enables individuals and collectives to orient themselves toward unresolved problems.

### 08 — Expanded Aesthetics

Aesthetics is not limited to the domain of artworks. It also concerns the conditions through which sensitivity, perception, experience, and the appearance of a world are configured. Expanded aesthetics investigates how bodies, objects, techniques, spaces, and practices organize the sensible field through which problems become perceptible, acquire form, and become capable of generating new orientations.

### 09 — Institution

Institutions can be understood as relatively stable forms of organization of relations, practices, and possibilities of existence. They are also modes through which collective problems are stabilized and temporarily resolved. This line investigates how institutions emerge, acquire consistency, produce identities and norms, and transform when the relations sustaining them no longer provide adequate resolutions to the problems of their field.
